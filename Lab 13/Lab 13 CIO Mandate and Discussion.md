Meridian Foundation Reserve Lab 13 Fictional Case - No AI

What CIO Said:

S1: Horizon seven years; annual rebalancing --> **Hard Constraint**; gives a time frame for the fund and requires rebalancing annually which limits freedom, but is necessary to the fund. 

S2: Objective: CPI + 4% a year, net of costs, over rolling 5-year periods --> **Objective**; gives a goal for the fund to hit the inflation rate (measured as CPI) in addition to 4% a year which is net of costs over 5 year rolling periods. This is a goal, not a constraint. 

S3: Pays out $0.75 million every quarter; twelve months of payouts held in cash or daily-liquid government securities --> **Hard Constraint**; this gives a minimum payout that needs to be met every quarter with payouts of 1 year held in cash or liquid cash-like assets. 

S4: Benchmark: 60% global equities, 40% U.S. investment-grade bonds. --> **Preference**; this is a rough benchmark of what the portfolio should hold and is not a hard constraint as the actual portfolio will have fluctuating weights and will not be exactly these weights. 

S5: Long-only: no borrowing, short sales or derivatives. --> **Hard Constraint**; this is a hard constraint because all positions must be long and there can be no other financial derivatives or tools used to help the portfolio. 

S6: No single issuer above 8%. --> **Hard Constraint**; this is a hard constraint because no position may be above 8%, likely to mitigize risk for this fund. 

S7: Annual turnover at most 25% without committee approval. --> **Hard Constraint**; this is a hard constraint because it does not allow the fund to restructure over 25% of its portfolio (selling and buying) without more approval from committee. 

S8: At least 90% redeemable within five business days. --> **Hard Constraint**; this limits the fund to how liquid it must be; assets must be easily liquidated within 5 business days and no portfolio holdings can be exempted. 

S9: "The reserve cannot have a calendar-year loss greater than 10%." No measure, no breach rule. --> **Unverifiable Promise**; this measure gives a hard limit but does not explain exactly what this means or how it is measured. 

S10: No sector, ESG, tax or home-bias preference; nobody has said what the pilot should optimize. --> Does not specify what the pilot should optimize which means there is missing info. There are no additional preferences stated.


Candidate D (All Cash Portfolio):
This candidate passes all of the mandate.py file checks, but the objective of CPI + 4% has no way of being proven to pass the S2 CIO mandate. Because the portfolio is only cash, the portfolio is at the will of inflation (CPI or similar) and it is extremely unlikely that the portfolio gains 4% on top of that because cash does not appreciate unless there is deflation. The benchmark of 60% global equities and 40% US investment grade bonds (S4) is also not close at all because 100% of the fund is held in cash. S6 is also not passing if you consider cash a "single issuer" as cash is 100% of the fund. 


One Question the CIO Must Answer:
What does a 10% calendar-year loss mean and how can we measure this loss? Does this include 10% of the portfolio being lost in a stock market crash like 2008? Is this measure inclusive of losses from active management fees or total value of the fund? 


Project 2 Mandate (My Client's Mandate) - AI Allowed

S1: Initial funding of $10 million, invested beginning now, for four children currently aged 8, 6, 4, and 2; the trust continues after the client's death. --> **Hard Constraint**; establishes the starting capital, beneficiaries, and continuation requirement.

S2: Support each child throughout their lifetime; the first 20 years are the initial planning period, ending when the youngest turns 22. --> **Objective and Horizon**; the trust's obligations extend beyond the initial planning period.

S3: Require a guaranteed nominal return of 10% annually. --> **Unverifiable Promise / Classroom Assumption**; 10% may be assumed for simplified calculations, but it cannot be presented as a guaranteed outcome of a risky investment portfolio.

S4: Use the S&P 500 Total Return Index as the return and risk comparison benchmark. --> **Preference**; reflects the client's desired comparison, without requiring the trust to hold the index's exact investments.

S5: Make $50,000 available annually per child for four college years, beginning at age 18 and ending immediately before age 22; total college funding is $200,000 per child. --> **Hard Constraint**; specifies the amount and timing of education payments.

S6: Beginning at each child's 22nd birthday, distribute $200,000 annually to that child for life, paid in equal monthly installments of $200,000 / 12, approximately $16,666.67. --> **Hard Constraint**; once all four qualify, combined distributions are $800,000 annually.

S7: Keep college and adult distributions fixed in nominal dollars, with no inflation adjustment. --> **Hard Constraint**; payment amounts remain unchanged, although their purchasing power may decline.

S8: Annualized portfolio return volatility must not exceed 25%. --> **Hard Constraint requiring a measurement definition**; the return frequency, measurement period, and breach response remain unspecified. This limit alone does not establish S&P 500-comparable risk.

S9: Annual portfolio turnover must not exceed 33%. --> **Hard Constraint**; limits trading, although the turnover calculation must be defined before implementation.

S10: Short sales and derivatives are permitted. --> **Investment Permission**; these strategies are allowed, but their use remains subject to the distribution, volatility, and turnover constraints.

**One question for the client:** If actual investment returns cannot be guaranteed, will you accept 10% as a return target while prioritizing the required payments and 25% volatility limit?
