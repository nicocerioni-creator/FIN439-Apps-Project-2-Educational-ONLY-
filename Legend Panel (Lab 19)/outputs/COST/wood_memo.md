I would watch Costco under this growth and reinvestment framework. That is a judgment about its fit with the framework, not a prediction that its business will deteriorate. The revised growth screen passes, and the supplied evidence supports an efficient retail operation, but it does not establish an exponential technology platform or a defensible learning curve. The annual filing describes low prices, purchasing scale, distribution efficiencies, and rapid inventory turnover. [Operating model](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983226000093/cost-20260830.htm)

Revenue increased from USD 226,954,000,000 at fiscal year end 2022-08-28 to USD 303,154,000,000 at fiscal year end 2026-08-30. The code prints revenue CAGR of 7.51%, above the 5% threshold, so the growth check passes. Latest ResearchAndDevelopmentExpense is not found in the eligible financial facts; the R&D / revenue check against 5% is not known. Missing disclosure does not mean no research spending or no innovation, and this accounting measure can disadvantage a retailer relative to a technology company.

The quarterly discussion describes digitally-enabled sales and online offerings, but that qualitative evidence does not establish transformative platform convergence, TAM expansion, or Wright's Law cost declines. Those assessments remain not known on the supplied evidence. [Digital operations and competitive conditions](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983226000051/cost-20260510.htm)

My biggest uncertainty is whether Costco's innovation is economically significant but poorly captured by the available R&D tag. Evidence of sustained growth meeting the coded requirement together with documented reinvestment in scalable innovation would strengthen the framework-fit judgment. I will not invent a price target, holdings position, or forecast to fill the gaps.

I trust the narrow growth-screen result because its inputs and threshold are explicit; I would not trust it as a complete assessment of business quality. A company can compound value without fitting a disruptive-growth mandate, and the missing R&D figure prevents a complete reinvestment comparison.

Threshold kept: 5% annual revenue growth, selected by the user to test a mature retailer; this is a sensitivity choice, not an empirically validated innovation hurdle.
